01

How this lesson works

To talk about food in French you almost always need a partitive article — du, de la, de l', des — meaning "some." First the theory, then the vocabulary from your slides, then practice using them together.

02

Parler des quantités

To say "some" of something — an unspecified amount — French uses de combined with the article, matching the noun's gender.

ArticleExemple
Masculin (de + le)duJe mange du riz.
Féminin (de + la)de laJe mange de la confiture.
Devant une voyellede l'Je bois de l'eau.
PlurieldesJe mange des pâtes.

Same idea as un/une/des for countable things — but for food and drink, you usually can't count it, so du/de la/de l' mark "some of it" instead.

03

La négation

In a negative sentence, every partitive article — du, de la, de l', des — collapses to a plain de (or d' before a vowel).

AffirmativeNégativeExemple
du→ deJe ne mange pas de riz.
de la→ deJe ne mange pas de confiture.
de l'→ d'Je ne bois pas d'eau.
des→ deJe ne mange pas de pâtes.
Why You're no longer talking about "some" of a specific amount — you're saying there's none at all, so the article that marked the quantity disappears.
