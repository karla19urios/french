My vision · designed by a polyglot

Learning a language is a journey, not a race.

I don't want to slow you down. I want to prevent you from wasting time.

01 — 02 · The urgency

“I need to learn this language. And I need to learn it fast.”

I understand it

For work. For school. For moving abroad. For life. Wanting to progress quickly isn't a problem — it can actually be one of your greatest motivations.

But there is a difference

Between fast progress and instant fluency. You can accelerate the process. You cannot skip the process.

03 — 04 · More lessons ≠ more fluency

Your brain doesn't learn by the hour.

10, 20, 30 lessons a month

More lessons can give you more opportunities to learn. They don't automatically make everything stick. Learning happens through exposure, repetition, practice and time.

You build a system

You don't memorize a language and simply “spit it out”. Something you understand today may take time before you can use it naturally. That's normal.

Understand→Practice→Forget→Review→Use→Automate

05 — 06 · Knowing ≠ being able to use

“When am I ever going to use this?”

Everything connects

Not everything you learn has to feel immediately useful. Some things are useful today. Some become useful later. And some prepare you for what comes next. A language isn't a list of isolated things to memorize.

Different stages

I can understand it. I can recognize it. I can explain the rule. And still not be able to use it spontaneously. That's normal.

Understanding→Recall→Production→Automaticity

07 · Progress isn't linear

Forgetting doesn't always mean you didn't learn.

Learn→Forget→Review→Recognize→Forget again→Understand→Practice→Use

Sometimes your knowledge simply hasn't become automatic yet.

And sometimes progress is happening before you can actually feel it.

08 — 09 · Build. Don't restart.

Feeling stuck doesn't always mean the methodology isn't working.

Teacher→Textbook→App→Method→Teacher→Method…

Constantly changing makes you repeat things you've already studied. You may spend your time starting over instead of building forward.

Changing your methodology can be useful when there's a real reason. Changing constantly because progress doesn't feel fast enough can slow you down.

Sometimes the fastest way forward is to stop restarting.

10 · What is wasting time?

If you want to progress as fast as realistically possible, know what actually wastes your time.

  • Constantly changing methodologies.
  • Rushing toward C1 before your foundations are strong.
  • Starting A1 again with every new teacher.
  • Abandoning something because you can't use it immediately.
  • Memorizing without context.
  • Expecting yesterday's lesson to become automatic overnight.
  • Taking endless lessons without practicing between them.

Learning takes time. Restarting takes time too.

Levels · A note on C1

Studying the theory up to C1 is not the same as mastering the four skills.

Many courses promise C1 as if it were fluency in everything. But covering all the theory of a level is not the same as owning it: you can study every rule up to C1 and still not be able to produce at C1. Very often the opposite happens — a gap opens between what you can understand and what you can actually use.

What you understand

ListeningReading

Grows fastest. Exposure alone keeps feeding it, so it runs ahead of everything else.

The gap

What you can produce

SpeakingWriting

Grows only with use, mistakes, correction and time. Rushing through levels widens the distance instead of closing it.

The goal isn't to reach C1 on paper. It's to close the gap between what you understand and what you can use.

12 — 13 · My own journey

I've been speaking French for 6 years. Not 3 months. Not 30 lessons.

Six years of exposure, practice, mistakes, repetition and real use. And yes: I love the language. That matters too, because enjoying a language makes you want to come back to it.

My advice isn't just a teaching philosophy. It comes from learning multiple languages, trying different methods, making mistakes, finding what works — and staying with the process.

Motivation can accelerate the journey. It can't eliminate the journey.

You don't need to learn slowly. You need to learn intentionally. Take advantage of your motivation, practice consistently, use your time outside the classroom, trust the process, build on what you already know.
